# Loaded stability trade study

Generated by `node scripts/tip_trade.mjs` from `src/sim/stability.js`. Do not edit by hand. Review item 1.

## The problem (as built at 7729e75)

A 5000 kg rover on a 3.92 x 3.84 m wheel footprint (wheelbase x track) carries the 14940 kg, 12.192 m module with its centre at 4.5 m
(module bottom 3.00 m: seat 2.595 m + carry ride 0.408 m). The combined centre of gravity is **3.63 m** up.
Static tip angle: **27.9° about the wheel centre line (31.5° about the outer tyre edge, which is what `rover.js` reports), 28.3° nose-down.**
A lateral acceleration of 0.35 m/s² (the loaded limit) leans the resultant by atan(a / g_moon) = 12.2° because lunar g is only 1.62 m/s².
On the 15° design slope that leaves 0.7° (needs ≥ 8°). The autonomy therefore stops the loaded rover at a 14° tilt, which is where the last M6 run failed.

## Requirement (stated on the design slope)

* Static tip angle, loaded, ≥ 35° (the choice aims 38°) about the wheel centre line (target 35–45°; planetary rovers such as MSL are about 45° [from memory, verify]), in roll and in pitch.
* On the 15° design slope, after the 12.2° manoeuvre lean: ≥ 8° left. Both conditions are the same number: 35° = 8 + 15 + 12.2.
* Constraints: overall width ≤ 5.6 m (track ≤ 4.8 m; the port vestibule is 6 m wide), wheelbase inside the 4.9 m frame (≤ 4.8 m), module bottom ≥ 1.2 m (above the 1.06 m wheel tops), module end approach / departure angle ≥ 20° (design slope + 5°).
